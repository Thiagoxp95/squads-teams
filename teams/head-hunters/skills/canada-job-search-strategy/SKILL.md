# Canada Job Search Strategy (Brazilian PM)

## 1. Intake (ask once, then keep in a profile note)
Immigration/work status and dates (PR, open work permit, closed permit, in process) · target
cities/remote · languages (English level; French?) · PM specialism (B2B SaaS, fintech, marketplace,
growth, platform) · seniority and years · domains · salary floor (CAD) · runway in months.
Never guess status — it drives which roles are realistic.

## 2. Targeting
- Markets: Toronto/GTA (largest tech, fintech, banks), Waterloo region (startups, Communitech),
  Vancouver (SaaS, gaming, climate), Montréal (AI, gaming — French usually needed; Quebec's
  Charter of the French Language makes French the norm at work), Ottawa (gov-tech, Shopify
  alumni), Calgary (energy-tech, fintech). Remote-Canada roles widen the pool.
- Titles to target (Canada uses US-style titles): Product Manager, Senior PM, Product Owner,
  Technical PM, Group PM. Consider one step down in title for the first role if the trade-off is
  a strong brand or domain match — decide explicitly, don't drift.
- Pick 2 lanes max (e.g. fintech PM in Toronto + B2B SaaS PM remote-Canada). Build a target list
  of 40–60 companies across tiers: dream / realistic / safe.

## 3. Positioning the Brazilian background as an asset
- Translate scale: Brazil is a large, complex market (high-volume consumers, Pix, inflation,
  regulatory complexity). Lead with users, revenue and growth numbers.
- One-line context for every Brazilian employer ("Nubank — digital bank, 100M+ customers").
- Assets to emphasise: Latin America expansion, Portuguese/Spanish, multicultural teams,
  doing a lot with limited resources.
- Address "Canadian experience" proactively by showing local relevance (Canadian clients, remote
  work with North American teams, local volunteering/courses). In Ontario, employers with 25+
  staff may not require "Canadian experience" in public postings (ESA, from 2026-01-01) and the
  OHRC treats strict requirements as discrimination — know this, but lead with value, not law.

## 4. Channel mix (the hidden job market is real)
Target split of effort: ~50% networking and referrals · ~30% targeted applications (≤ 10/week,
each tailored) · ~20% skills/visibility (LinkedIn posts, meetups, a portfolio case study).
Referrals convert far better than cold applications — every application to a target company
should also have a warm contact attempt.

## 5. Weekly operating rhythm
Mon: scout + update pipeline (job-pipeline-tracker) · Tue–Thu: 5–10 tailored outreach messages,
2–3 informational chats, 5–10 applications · Fri: interview prep + weekly review.
Weekly review metrics: applications → screens (target ≥ 10–15%), outreach → reply (target
≥ 25%), chats → referrals. Change one variable at a time when a rate is low.

## 6. Use free support
IRCC-funded settlement services are free for permanent residents and approved PRs (outside
Quebec), including pre-arrival employment help; mentorship programs such as TRIEC (Toronto) and
ACCES/Achēv speed-mentoring; Brazil-Canada Chamber of Commerce and Brazilian professional groups;
ProductTank / Product Camp meetups in each city.

## 7. Delegation
Job scout → roles + pipeline · Networker → contacts + chats · Résumé writer → tailored CV/letter
per role · Interview coach → prep per interview. The lead reviews quality and the weekly numbers,
and protects the candidate's energy (job search is a marathon; plan rest).

## Guardrails
Never misstate work authorization, titles, dates or metrics. Never submit applications or send
messages without the candidate's explicit approval.
