# Canadian Interview Prep (Product Manager)

## Typical PM loop in Canada
Recruiter screen (20–30 min) → hiring manager (45–60) → product sense/case or take-home →
execution/analytics → cross-functional panel (eng, design) → final/values or exec round →
reference checks (2–3 references, ideally recent managers; English-speaking Brazilian references
are fine — brief them first) → offer.

## Prep pack per interview
1. What this round tests (3–5 competencies) and who the interviewers are (LinkedIn research).
2. Company brief: product, customers, business model, recent news, competitors, values page.
3. Likely questions for this round, most likely first; map each to a story in the star-story-bank.
4. Product sense case: clarify goal and user → segment users → pain points → prioritise →
   solutions → trade-offs → success metrics. Execution: define metrics (north star + guardrails),
   diagnose a metric drop (internal vs external, segment, funnel step).
5. "Tell me about yourself" (60–90 s): present (role/scope) → past highlights (2 results) → why
   Canada/why this company → what you want next.
6. "Why Canada?": positive and forward-looking (career, family, settled plans); show commitment.
7. 3–5 sharp questions to ask (team goals, how success is measured in 6 months, product
   decision-making, biggest challenge). Not asking questions reads as disinterest.
8. Gaps/landmines: Canadian experience, relocation, English, career gaps — honest, confident answers.

## Canadian norms
- Punctual: join video 5 min early; arrive 10–15 min early in person. Business casual unless told.
- Friendly small talk and first names are normal; informality is not a lack of seriousness.
- Be direct and concise; specific self-promotion with evidence is expected — modesty that hides
  your role costs offers. Use "I" for your contributions.
- It's fine to ask for a question to be repeated or to take a moment to think.
- Video: quiet, tidy background, tested camera/mic, look at the camera.

## Questions employers should not ask (Human Rights Codes)
Age, marital/family status, pregnancy/childcare, religion, place of birth, ethnic origin,
disability not tied to job requirements, citizenship (they may ask whether you are legally entitled
to work in Canada), and in Ontario requiring "Canadian experience". Coach a calm redirect:
"I'm fully able to meet the requirements of this role — happy to talk about how I've done X."

## Salary
Research: posting range (required in Ontario for employers with 25+ staff, and in BC), Job Bank
wage data by occupation and region, levels.fyi/Glassdoor. Give a range anchored by your target;
discuss base + bonus + equity + benefits (health, RRSP matching, vacation). Delay specifics until
scope is clear if possible.

## After the interview
Thank-you email within 24 hours to each interviewer, referencing something specific. If no news
after the stated timeline (or about a week), follow up once politely. If rejected, ask for brief
feedback. In Ontario, employers (25+ staff) must tell interviewed candidates the decision within
45 days. Log everything in the tracker.
